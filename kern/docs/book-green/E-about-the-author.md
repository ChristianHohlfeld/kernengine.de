# About the Author

![Christian Heinrich Hohlfeld](../buch/bilder/autor.jpg)

**Christian Heinrich Hohlfeld** is a software developer in Konstanz, Germany. He has been
programming since 2010 – in C++, Java, C#/.NET, TypeScript and Python – and studied software
engineering at HTWG Konstanz (B.Sc.). His approach: first understand how a piece of work really
runs, then build the whole system until it is live. Routine may be taken over by software; the
decisions stay with people.

Games and 3D have been with him from the start. His Android app MMA News, built on his own and
published from 2010, reached around 220,000 downloads. In 2015 came Alien Star Racing, a game with
procedurally generated levels on his own toolkit on top of cocos2d-x (C++); it made the top ten in a
competition sponsored by Microsoft. With VoxelShaper he is developing a voxel CAD for 3D printing in
the browser, and with kern3d a WebGL core with no dependencies – the forerunner of Kern.

Professionally, he has developed software for billing and financing (ines GmbH), a platform for
skilled trades (wirsindhandwerk.de) and, as a freelancer, for clients including Robert Bosch GmbH.
Today he works in Konstanz as an advisor for digital transformation and process optimization and
leads projects on digitalization and artificial intelligence. Alongside this he builds and runs his
own products, among them digitalisierungsplanung.de, a tool that records real workflows in the
browser and makes them accessible to AI agents through an MCP server.

## How Kern and this book came about

Kern and this book were made with artificial intelligence, and nobody should have to find that out
by reading between the lines. Most of the source code and the text was written by an AI system
(Claude by Anthropic), under the author's direction.

**With the author lie:**

- **The idea and the goal:** an engine in pure C++ that runs in the browser on phone and desktop
  equally fast and looking the same, with an editor, a robot simulation and its own training
  system for neural networks.
- **The architecture and the choice of building blocks:** C++20 to WebAssembly and WebGL2, Box3D
  for physics, the Micro Duck with Pollen Robotics' original networks instead of re-created
  movements, faithfulness to the real robot before tricks of its own, the licensing model.
- **The standard and the decisions:** what is good enough, what is thrown out and what goes live.
  He tested every feature on real devices; many improvements came out of this testing.
- **The responsibility** for software and book.

**With the AI lie:** the implementation in code and tests, the research in sources and technical
publications, the writing and translating of the chapters – at a pace that a single person doesn't
reach without such tools.

The result shows what is possible today: an experienced developer who knows what he wants to build
and makes the right decisions builds with AI, in a short time, what used to take a team. The AI
writes; what gets built, and whether it is any good, is decided by a human.
